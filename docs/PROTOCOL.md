# Nudge engine protocol v1

Transport: one UTF-8 JSON object per line on a bundled child process’s private stdin/stdout. No network. Calls are serialized by `EngineBridge`; screen content is never logged. The Swift transport supplies `version: 1` to every command. Responses are `{ "ok": true, "data": ... }` or `{ "ok": false, "error": "..." }`.

Commands:

- `hello`: protocol and capability metadata.
- `prepare`: `request` (unique ID), `observation`, `goal`, `progress`, and for tours optionally `scope` (`all`, `web` for a browser's page, or `app` for the browser's own controls), `region` (a chosen area as fractions of the observed frame, which makes the scope `area`), `limit` (most tour stops, 4 to 80, default 36) and `group` (group similar controls, default true). Returns an in-memory cached session or compact `prompt` for the platform inference adapter. Replaces pending interpretation identity.
- Tours (no goal): `prepare` also returns `batches`, one prompt per group of up to 6 controls. The tour covers up to 36 unique controls, grouped by part of the window and read top to bottom, with a labeled area (a sidebar, a toolbar) introducing its own controls. `install` then takes only the overview (`title`, `introduction`, `conclusion`) and creates placeholder steps with `ready: false`. `explain` takes `request`, `batch` and `steps` (`target` is the item's number within the batch, plus `title` and `explanation`), fills those steps, and gives any the model skipped a factual description built from the observed label and details. A tour is cached once every step is ready.
- Area tours (`prepare` with `region`): after the area's controls come its pictures (elements with role `picture` and source `vision`, whose `detail` reads `looks like: …`), then its text gathered into blocks (headings, paragraphs; at most 8) with role `AXTextBlock`. Pictures and text blocks are found in a screenshot of the area, so later reads can't find them again: their steps keep the position where they were found and report `target_source: "area"`.
- `install`: matching `request` and generated `plan`. Validates the step count, evidence, nonduplicate observed target indices, and nonempty explanations. Converts compact model indices to observed anchors. Rejects stale responses. Goal guides are restricted to one visible action; general tours cannot request actions.
- `observe`: a fresh `observation`. Returns `step`, `missing`, `changed`, `complete`, or `idle`, plus the session and resolved bounds. No cached coordinates are used when this command receives a current observation.
- `next`, `back`, `replay`: update the session index. `goto` with `index` jumps to a step. Returned bounds are where the step was last verified; the client shows them at once and then observes again to confirm.
- `cancel`: discard pending interpretation.
- `clear`: clear pending interpretation, session and memory cache.

An observation has `app`, `bundle`, `window`, `window_id`, `elements`, and optional `page` (the web address without query or fragment). The observation may also carry `scene`: what a chosen area's picture seems to show, from on-device image classification. Each element includes a unique `id`, visible `label`, `role`, `source` (`ax` for Accessibility, `ocr` for recognized text, `vision` for a picture found in a screenshot), optional `context` (the named part of the window, such as `Toolbar` or `Sidebar`), optional `detail` (a link's destination such as `goes to /pricing on this site`, a button's `tip: …`, a checkbox's `on`/`off`, a pop-up's `shows …`) and `bounds` (`x`, `y`, `width`, `height`). Bounds are fractions of the adapter’s current reference rectangle, using a **top-left** origin. The Mac reference rectangle is the focused window; for goal guidance it is the union of the window and the app menu bar. Observations can carry several hundred elements. `prepare` chooses a spatially balanced subset of at most 60 (controls first, visible text after, spread across a 3×3 grid of the window) and numbers it in reading order for the model; `observe` resolves targets against the full observation. The reference rectangle remains in the native adapter. Rust has no Apple handles or objects.

A plan has `title`, `introduction`, `conclusion`, `steps` (at most 8; tours are sorted top to bottom, then left to right) and `continues`. Steps whose target can't be verified or repeats are dropped rather than rejecting the plan. State responses include `target_source` so the client knows whether tracking needs OCR. A step has `title`, `explanation`, numeric-string `target` (before installation), `action`, and `evidence`. The adapter supplies generated structured data; the engine validates it. A Windows inference adapter can return exactly the same plan schema.

The engine does not declare action success from timing or screen navigation. The client sends `next` only for explanation advancement, explicit confirmation, or explicit skipping. For ongoing user goals, the client adds a compact user-confirmed progress summary and requests another plan from a fresh observation. Skipping an ongoing task pauses rather than fabricating completion.

The cache is bounded to 12 introductory tours, expires after 20 minutes and lives only in memory. Its key includes app, window title, goal, and current label/role/ID structure. Cache reuse still requires fresh observation. A unique matching label/role/source may be reacquired after reordering; ambiguous repeated controls require an exact ID. Losing a target or substantially changing the structure hides its highlight.
